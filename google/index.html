Google for Education · Designed at Huge · 2025

One URL. Competing intents. Discovery failing as a page problem.

App Hub had grown past 108 integrations. Stakeholders asked for page-level fixes. The real work was rebuilding discovery as a multi-audience system under Google’s design system and a single shared URL.

Senior Product Designer · UX strategy, IA, interaction, high-fidelity · Embedded with product, marketing, engineering, content, and Google stakeholders

Before: sort-only catalog · After: orientation before inventory

Framing

Better sorting would not unlock discovery.

A heuristic audit across 20+ structural issues showed the failure modes: sort-only navigation, IA organized around Google’s offer rather than who arrives, opaque integration labels, and no above-the-fold audience paths. Every stakeholder entered the same undifferentiated flow.

Discoverability

108+ apps with sort-only navigation. Users could reorder the catalog—but not narrow it.

Information architecture

Organized around what Google offered, not around who arrived or what they needed to do.

Content clarity

Integration labels were technically accurate and operationally opaque—so most visitors could not filter by them.

Navigation

No above-the-fold paths for different intents. Institutions, educators, and partners shared one collision.

System relationships

Self-ID, teaching, editorial, and taxonomy as one field.

The visitor needs to see how the parts relate—not watch a morph from messy to clean. Add #focus to isolate the catalog spine.

Decision spine

01

Lead with self-identification

Instead of opening into a catalog, the landing asks who you are and what you need. Parallel entry points reduce the search space before options appear.

02

Teach before filtering

Filters only work if people understand the labels. Explainer cards turn technical integration types into concrete outcomes—then the catalog becomes usable.

03

Pair filters with editorial discovery

Filters assume intent. Many visitors arrive without it. Editorial curation sits beside the taxonomy so exploration and precision can coexist.

04

Order taxonomy by decision priority

Integration type, institution, subject, category, language—ordered by how buyers and educators evaluate apps. Pagination replaced endless scroll.

Impact

Structural ship. Metrics not yet.

Recently launched. Long-term metrics are not yet available; the current signal is structural—the discovery system is live on edu.google.com reflecting the diagnosis and decisions above. No outcome percentage is claimed.
