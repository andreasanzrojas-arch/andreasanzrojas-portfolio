Who is arriving

Framing

Better sorting would not unlock discovery.

App Hub had grown past 108 integrations. Stakeholders asked for page-level fixes. A heuristic audit across 20+ structural issues showed the failure: sort-only navigation, architecture organized around Google’s offer rather than who arrives, opaque integration labels, and no above-the-fold audience paths.

  • DiscoverabilityUsers could reorder the catalog. They could not narrow it.
  • ArchitectureOrganized around what Google offered, not around who arrived or what they needed to do.
  • ContentIntegration labels were technically accurate and operationally opaque.
  • NavigationInstitutions, educators, and partners shared one collision.

Ownership

Framing, diagnosis, multi-audience architecture.

Senior Product Designer. UX strategy, information architecture, interaction, and high-fidelity, embedded with product, marketing, engineering, content, and Google stakeholders. After UX handoff, engineering and visual design implemented in the Google design system.

The audience switch above is the decision: self-identification before inventory. Labels before filters. Editorial beside taxonomy. You can move through that sequence without reading this section.

Decisions

  • 01 · Self-identificationParallel entry points reduce the search space before options appear.
  • 02 · Teach before filteringExplainer cards turn technical integration types into outcomes. The dedicated explainer frame is not in the local cache; the filter taxonomy is.
  • 03 · Editorial beside precisionFilters assume intent. Many visitors arrive without it. Curation sits with the taxonomy.
  • 04 · Decision-priority orderIntegration type, institution, subject, category, language — ordered by how buyers and educators evaluate. Pagination replaced endless scroll.

Impact

Structural ship. Metrics not yet.

Recently launched. Long-term metrics are not yet available. The signal is structural: the discovery system is live, reflecting the diagnosis above. No outcome percentage is claimed.
