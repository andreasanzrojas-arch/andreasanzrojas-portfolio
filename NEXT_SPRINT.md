# NEXT SPRINT — Case Study Content & Evidence

_Goal of this sprint: turn the three featured projects from provisional copy + abstract
artifacts into **real, evidence-backed executive product stories**._

**Out of scope (frozen):** visual direction, navigation, command palette, IA, section order.
This sprint is content + targeted artifact accuracy only — not a redesign.

**Working principle (apply to every phase):** the **seniority test** —
_"Would a Head of Design believe this came from real enterprise work?"_ If not, cut it.

**Shared deliverable for every project:** a tight executive narrative arc —
**Context → Problem → My role / scope → Key decisions → Outcome.** Lead with leadership and
decisions, not screens or process.

---

## Phase 1 — Banco de Bogotá · CDT (digital investing)

The strongest project (real, nameable, has a metric). Make it the proof anchor.

**Required information**
- The real before/after: what the analog CDT flow was, what the digital flow became.
- Andrea's exact role and scope (lead? IC? team size? stakeholders aligned?).
- The 12-step → 3-tap claim: confirm it's accurate or restate honestly.
- Business/risk/brand stakeholders involved and how alignment was achieved.

**Missing information**
- Verified metric for "+30% digital CDT openings" — confirm number, timeframe, and source.
- Any secondary outcomes (drop-off reduction, time-to-open, adoption).
- Constraints faced (regulatory, legacy systems, org politics).

**Assets needed**
- Real (or faithfully abstracted) before/after journey.
- Confirmation the service-blueprint artifact matches reality (stages, swimlanes, drop-off point).
- Banco de Bogotá logo (for credibility bar + project).

**Risks**
- Over-claiming the metric without a source undermines the whole portfolio's credibility.
- Showing too much process dilutes the leadership framing.

**Deliverables**
- Final CDT narrative (Context → Problem → Role → Decisions → Outcome).
- Verified headline metric + supporting metric(s).
- Accuracy-checked CDT blueprint artifact.

## Phase 2 — Mastercard · Global Payments (merchant onboarding)

Highest-prestige name; also highest NDA sensitivity. Credibility comes from systems thinking,
not screens.

**Required information**
- What Andrea actually owned in merchant onboarding / payment adoption.
- The compliance/risk dimension (KYC, sanctions, etc.) she designed around.
- Scope of "global scale" — regions, teams, or rollout breadth (whatever is shareable).

**Missing information**
- Any shareable outcome or proxy metric (currently "Global" placeholder — weak).
- Exactly what can be named vs. must stay anonymized under NDA.

**Assets needed**
- NDA-safe architecture/flow that is accurate in *shape* without exposing IP.
- Mastercard logo (Mastercard can be named; client stays anonymized).
- Confirmation the payments-architecture artifact (KYC gate, acquisition flow, layers) is faithful.

**Risks**
- NDA exposure — keep everything abstracted to system shape, no real screens or data.
- Weak proof: with no metric, the story must lean hard on complexity + decisions to feel senior.

**Deliverables**
- NDA-safe Mastercard narrative emphasizing enterprise payments + compliance leadership.
- A stronger proof signal than "Global" (qualitative scope statement if no metric is shareable).
- Accuracy-checked payments-architecture artifact.

## Phase 3 — Huge · Google for Education (product design + design systems)

This phase establishes the "Google quality bar" credibility. Lean on systems and platform thinking.

**Required information**
- What Andrea specifically designed/owned within Google for Education via Huge.
- The design-system / platform dimension (components, governance, scale).
- How working to Google's standards shaped her craft and process.

**Missing information**
- Any concrete outcome, adoption, or scale figure.
- What is shareable about Google work (likely tighter than the others).

**Assets needed**
- Design-system map / component-library evidence that is real in structure.
- Huge and/or Google for Education attribution (confirm how to credit appropriately).
- Confirmation the design-system artifact (dependency stack, governed library, coverage) is faithful.

**Risks**
- "Worked at Google's bar via an agency" can read as distant — framing must make her ownership clear.
- Confidentiality around Google work may limit specifics.

**Deliverables**
- Google/Huge narrative emphasizing design systems, platform thinking, and craft at scale.
- Accuracy-checked design-system artifact.
- Clear, honest attribution model (Huge → Google for Education).
