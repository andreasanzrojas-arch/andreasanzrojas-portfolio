Research

Comprehension anxiety—not product complexity—was the barrier.

The opportunity was not merely digitizing twelve steps. It was redesigning the sequence of information so people could understand, decide, and commit without a teller.

Comprehension first

Anxiety about locking savings without understanding outcomes blocked commitment more than UI polish.

Auto-renewal surprise

Most users discovered auto-renewal after maturity—never during opening. A major complaint surface.

Advisor gap

Constraints

Bank design system and brand were given. Architecture was the work. Risk and compliance co-designed every trust barrier.

Contested control

Auto-renewal as explicit customer choice.

Making renewal an explicit toggle was one of the most debated decisions in the project. It required alignment across Product, Compliance, and business stakeholders to balance transparency, regulation, and retention—before approval.

Approved pattern: a clear toggle at confirmation, with continued in-app control for active CDTs. Confirmation email closes the loop with a manage-renewal link. Stillness is intentional—this is governance, not theater.

Decision spine

01

3-step digital flow

Reorganized around the customer’s decision—not the bank’s operating sequence. Value and comprehension come first; required checks stay after the decision feels grounded.

02

Simulate before identity

Interactive simulator first so users decide with concrete numbers before committing. Post-launch: simulation had the highest engagement and lowest drop-off of any screen (Hotjar / funnels; directional, no %).

03

Transparent conditions

Rate, term, conditions, and legal appear as a readable summary on confirmation—not buried in a separate document. Trust through visibility at the decision point.

04

Auto-renewal transparency

Explicit toggle + continued app control. Complaints decreased (soft; no invented %). Email includes manage link so transparency does not end at confirm.
